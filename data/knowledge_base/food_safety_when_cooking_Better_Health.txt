Food safety when cooking
Source: Better Health Channel (Victorian Government)

SUMMARY
- Cook food properly – to at least 75°C or hotter.
- Use a thermometer to check the temperature of cooked foods.
- If you use a microwave, check that the food is cooked evenly throughout.
- Cook foods made from eggs thoroughly.
- Cool and store cooked food as soon as possible.
- Reheat food until steaming hot.

The way food is cooked is as important as the way it is prepared and stored. Inadequate cooking is a common cause of food poisoning. Cross-contamination from raw to cooked foods, such as from hands, chopping boards or utensils, can also cause food poisoning. Most foods, especially meat, poultry, fish and eggs, should be cooked thoroughly to kill most types of food poisoning bacteria.

In general, food should be cooked to a temperature of at least 75°C or hotter. When food is cooked, it should be eaten promptly, kept hotter than 60°C, or cooled, covered and stored in the fridge or freezer.

PEOPLE AT RISK FROM FOOD POISONING
Some people are more at risk from food poisoning than others. Vulnerable groups include: young children, pregnant women, older people, and people with weakened immune systems. Special care should be taken when preparing, cooking, serving and storing food for these groups.

HIGH-RISK FOODS AND THE TEMPERATURE DANGER ZONE
Food poisoning bacteria grow more easily on some foods than others. High-risk foods include raw and cooked meat and poultry, dairy products, eggs, seafood, and cooked rice and pasta. Bacteria multiply fastest between 5°C and 60°C — this range is known as the "temperature danger zone." High-risk foods should not be left in this temperature range for more than a combined total of 2 hours (after which they should be used immediately) or up to 4 hours (after which they must be thrown away). This is sometimes referred to as the 2-hour/4-hour rule.

COOK ALL FOOD TO A TEMPERATURE OF 75°C
Cook food properly to at least 75°C or hotter. Use a thermometer to check the temperature of cooked foods. If using a microwave, check the food is cooked evenly throughout — microwaves can heat unevenly, so stir or rotate food partway through cooking and let it stand before checking temperature.

FOOD SAFETY WITH RAW EGGS
Cook foods made from eggs thoroughly. Avoid recipes that use raw or lightly cooked eggs (such as some mayonnaise, mousse or dessert recipes) unless made with pasteurised egg products, as raw eggs can carry Salmonella bacteria.

FOOD SAFETY AND MICROWAVE COOKING
When cooking or reheating in a microwave, ensure food reaches a safe temperature throughout, not just on the outside edges. Follow the same food safety actions as with other cooking methods to ensure food is heated evenly to above 75°C.

COOLING AND STORING FOOD
Cool and store cooked food as soon as possible after cooking. Splitting large batches of food into smaller portions helps it cool faster and reduces the time it spends in the temperature danger zone. Store cooled food covered in the fridge or freezer.

REHEAT FOOD TO STEAMING HOT
Reheat food until it is steaming hot – above 75°C or, preferably, boiling. Food should steam throughout, not just on the edges. Take care when reheating food in a microwave oven, and follow the same actions as when cooking with a microwave to ensure all the food is heated evenly to above 75°C.
